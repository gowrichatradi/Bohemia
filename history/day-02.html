Day 2 · Tue 29 Sep · Copenhagen

Copenhagen — the full day

The day the trip proper begins. Everything on it — the zoo, the market halls, the tower — is somewhere between mid-19th-century and medieval.

Copenhagen Zoo

Founded 1859 · Frederiksberg

The zoo opened in 1859 and is one of the oldest in Europe. It was founded by ornithologist Niels Kjærbølling on land loaned by the crown, and moved to its current Frederiksberg site the same year. The Norman Foster–designed Elephant House opened in 2008, and the Arctic Ring — the acrylic tunnel through the polar-bear pool — in 2013.

Speciality the Arctic Ring is the only exhibit of its kind in Scandinavia, and one of very few anywhere with an underwater walk-through beneath swimming polar bears. Melbourne has no polar bears at all — climate rules them out.

Torvehallerne

Opened 2011 · Nørreport

The market halls are new — they opened in September 2011 on the site of a former open-air market that had run at Israels Plads since the 19th century. Two glass halls hold about 80 stalls between them, from smørrebrød counters to spice merchants. Coffee Collective — the roastery that started Nordic light roasting in 2008 — runs a bar inside.

Speciality Denmark's smørrebrød tradition dates to the 19th-century labourer's lunch — rye bread as a plate, topped generously. The Hallernes Smørrebrød stall inside serves the traditional versions.

Rosenborg Castle

Built 1606–1633 · Dutch Renaissance

Christian IV — the "builder king" — built Rosenborg in stages as a summer palace in the countryside outside town. The Long Hall on the top floor is a rare survivor: coronation thrones with narwhal-tusk decoration, silver lions on guard, and interiors barely changed since the 17th century. When the royal family moved to Christiansborg in the 18th century, Rosenborg became the treasury — which is why the Danish Crown Jewels are still kept in its basement vault.

Notice the throne of the queen is made from silver from Kongsberg in Norway, and the king's throne from narwhal tusks — passed off as unicorn horn at a time when everyone knew what that meant.

Rundetårn (Round Tower)

Completed 1642 · Christian IV

Christian IV built the Round Tower as an astronomical observatory attached to Trinitatis Church — the astronomer Tycho Brahe had died in 1601 leaving no working observatory in Denmark. It is the oldest functioning astronomical observatory in Europe. A 209-metre spiral ramp — not stairs — winds around a hollow core, wide enough that Peter the Great supposedly rode a horse up it in 1716.

Speciality the ramp itself. Almost no other tower in Europe uses one — it was built this way so heavy astronomical instruments could be wheeled to the top. Pram-friendly by pure accident of the 17th century.

The canal boats from Nyhavn

Route follows medieval and 17th-c. waterways

The one-hour loop threads through Christianshavn (dug 1618 by Christian IV as a Dutch-style planned town), past the Opera and the Little Mermaid, and under bridges some of which are 17th-century. Christianshavn was a defensive addition — a walled Dutch town on an artificial island guarding the approach to the harbour — and its grid still shows.

Notice Vor Frelsers Kirke's spiral spire — the church of Christianshavn, 1752, with an external staircase you can climb around a golden spire. Visible from the boat.
