Day 21 · Sun 18 Oct · Home

The long haul, one last thing to notice

Not much left to see — but the airport you fly out of, and the flight itself, both have stories.

Did you knowKastrup's Terminal 3 was one of the world's first airport terminals to use wood as its primary flooring material — Danish oak, planed and oiled — because studies showed passengers walk slower and feel calmer on wood. Every airport with a "wooden concourse" copied it: Oslo Gardermoen, Helsinki-Vantaa, Zurich's E, and eventually parts of Melbourne Terminal 2.

Copenhagen Airport (Kastrup)

Opened 20 April 1925 · one of Europe's oldest civilian airports

Kastrup began as a grass strip on the flat coastal land east of Copenhagen and took its first regular flights in April 1925. It has been in continuous civilian operation since — surviving the German occupation, the postwar jet transition, and the Øresund Bridge rail integration in 2000, which put a train platform beneath the terminal. Its Terminal 3 is one of the most-praised transit terminals in Europe and hosts the driverless M2 metro you rode in on.

Notice the terminal's wooden floors — Danish oak, unusual for an international airport — are a deliberate design decision to soften the transit experience. Kastrup pioneered the "airport as living room" idea copied around the world.

Singapore Airlines SQ351

Copenhagen–Singapore–Melbourne · Kangaroo Route inheritance

The Copenhagen–Singapore route follows the pattern of the original "Kangaroo Route" — the multi-stop flight path that first connected Europe to Australia in 1947 (BOAC and Qantas). Modern flights collapse the stops to one; SQ's Copenhagen leg is a segment on that same geographic corridor. Singapore Changi's Terminal 3, where you'll transit, opened in 2008 with a "green wall" of 900 plants — one of the earliest airport biophilic installations.

Notice the descent into Melbourne over Port Phillip Bay — you cross the coast north of Torquay, over the Otways ranges, then over Werribee. The final approach over Bass Strait gives you the You Yangs and Geelong to the west; the CBD is under the right wing.

Melbourne — home

Founded 1835 · you live here

Nothing to add. You already know it. But after 20 nights of red-painted rorbuer, Baroque bridges, Gothic cathedrals and salt mines older than the pyramids — the tram lines, the redbrick houses and the eucalypts will look, for about 48 hours, like a completely different country.

Speciality the jetlag home is worse than the jetlag out. Give yourselves a day.

On screen · one for the flight

Amélie

IMDb 8.3 · 2001 · Jean-Pierre Jeunet

Not an airport film — but the definitive 'small pleasures make a life' film. After three weeks of new cities and monuments, Amélie is the right note to come home on: a reminder that the everyday street corner is where the story actually happens.

The Straight Story

IMDb 7.7 · 1999 · David Lynch

An older man crosses a continent on a lawnmower to see his estranged brother. Almost nothing happens. It is the anti-thriller — the film to watch when you've just returned from an epic trip and the tempo of home feels wrong. Rated G, made by Lynch, and one of the few films that will make you look forward to Melbourne quiet.

Up in the Air

IMDb 7.4 · 2009 · Jason Reitman · George Clooney

The definitive airport-and-flying movie. Almost every airport interior sequence — check-in, security, the pull-through concourse — was filmed at St. Louis Lambert. The film runs at exactly the pace of a long-haul, and hits differently after 20 nights on the road.
