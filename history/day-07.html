Day 7 · Sun 4 Oct · Svolvær & Henningsvær

The village on the rocks

Svolvær is the administrative centre; Henningsvær is the postcard. Both were built on cod, and both are still trying to work out what to be when the cod season ends in April.

Did you knowHenningsvær's football pitch has no dressing rooms and no fixed goal-posts — the wind blows too hard. Local team Henningsvær IL — nicknamed "Fisk & Cod" — used to store their goalposts inside the Trevarefabrikken and carry them out for each match. Aerial photography of the pitch went viral around 2015 and doubled the village's summer traffic.

Henningsvær

Fishing station since medieval times · football pitch 1988

Henningsvær is spread across half a dozen skerries connected by low bridges. It was one of the busiest cod bases in Lofoten by the 19th century — a floating population of thousands in winter — and its harbour was too narrow to build on, so buildings crept out onto every islet with a foothold. The football pitch, built in 1988 by carving flat rock and pouring concrete, is now one of the most photographed sports grounds in the world.

Notice the pitch has no seating and no permanent goals — the surrounding rock is grandstand enough. Local team Henningsvær IL still play matches on it in summer.

KaviarFactory

Former Norwegian Kaviar plant · gallery since 2013

The factory made cod-roe caviar — a Nordic staple — until it closed in the 1990s. Venke Hoff and Rolf Hoff bought the building in 2013 and opened it as a contemporary art gallery, showing work by international artists in industrial spaces designed for fish processing. It is one of the most unusual gallery spaces in northern Europe.

Speciality the exhibitions rotate, but the fixed installations — including a piece by Anish Kapoor — are on year-round. The building has direct water access on its seaward wall.

Engelskmannsbrygga

"The Englishman's Wharf" · working studio

The name goes back to the 18th century, when English merchants ran a trading post here for the cod trade with Britain and the Netherlands. The wharf is now a joint ceramics and glass studio, with the potters and glassblowers working in view of the harbour they still supply.

Notice the ceramics tradition here is a modern one — mid-20th-century — but the address, and the timber structure of the wharf, is 1700s.

Trevarefabrikken

Woodworks 1949–2005 · café/bar/sauna since 2015

The Henningsvær Woodworks made doors, window frames and boat interiors for Lofoten's construction boom from 1949 to 2005. It sat empty for a decade until three friends from Oslo restored it as a café, bar, hostel and harbourside sauna. The interior kept its industrial fittings, and the sauna at the end of the pier is heated by wood cut on site.

Speciality the seawater sauna — a wooden bath house at the end of the pier where you can plunge straight into the Vestfjord. Booking is often required.

Vestpollen chapel ruins

Chapel built 1898 · ruined 1953

A small wooden chapel built to serve a scatter of fishing families on the north shore of Austnesfjorden. It burned down in 1953 and was never rebuilt. The stone foundation and iron cross remain on a spit facing due north — which is why aurora photographers have adopted it.

Notice the site is unmarked and easy to drive past; the turn-off is about twenty minutes east of Svolvær on the E10.

For the little one

The three-year-old's version of today

Henningsvær's football pitch is the surprise hit. You walk out onto the concrete slab in the middle of black rock, look at the goalposts, look at the sea below — and she gets it immediately. Local kids sometimes have a match on Sunday afternoons; ask at Trevarefabrikken.

For the drive · 99% Invisible — 'Fish Stories'

podcast · Roman Mars

The show's short episodes on fishing towns, harbours, and northern light-culture all fit Lofoten. Search their archive for 'fisheries' or 'north sea' before the day begins.

On screen · Svolvær & Henningsvær

The Innocents (De uskyldige)

IMDb 7.0 · 2021 · Eskil Vogt

Norwegian-language horror about children with telepathic powers — filmed in Oslo, but its aesthetic (long summer light, mid-century apartment blocks against alpine sky) belongs to the same Nordic-noir cinematography school that keeps returning to Henningsvær.

Twin

IMDb 7.1 · 2019 · Kristofer Hivju · NRK

Filmed almost entirely between Svolvær, Kabelvåg and Henningsvær. Two identical twins, one of whom takes over the other's life — the fictional village is a composite of the places you're walking today.

Løvekvinnen (The Lion Woman)

IMDb 6.7 · 2016 · Vibeke Idsøe

Based on the Erik Fosnes Hansen novel. Filmed in northern Norway with sequences in Svolvær harbour. A young girl born covered in hair grows up in a Norwegian railway town in the early 20th century — a strange, beautiful film.
