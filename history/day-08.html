Day 8 · Mon 5 Oct · Svolvær → Oslo

Out of Lofoten, into the capital

A short evening in Oslo, on foot from the station. Most of what you'll see is 21st-century — the city has done more building this decade than in the previous fifty.

Did you knowThe Opera House's roof is walkable at all hours, in every season — it was designed so people could sled down it in winter. Snøhetta specifically tested the marble against sled runners and cross-country ski edges. Locals do use it.

Oslo Opera House

Completed 2008 · Snøhetta

Snøhetta's Opera House on the Bjørvika waterfront was the largest cultural building in Norway when it opened in 2008. The sloping white marble roof rising out of the fjord is not a decorative feature — it is a public plaza, walkable at all hours, and was the concept that won the design competition. The building won the Mies van der Rohe Award in 2009.

Speciality the marble is Italian — La Facciata Carrara — chosen for the quality of its snow-white light. Under moonlight the roof glows blue.

Bjørvika & the Barcode

Redeveloped from freight docks 2005–2020

Everything east of Oslo Central Station along the water was container port and railway sidings until 2005. The Barcode row of thin towers, the new Munch Museum (opened 2021), and the Deichman library (2020) all sit on filled land or former dockside. Oslo shifted its whole cultural centre of gravity to Bjørvika in fifteen years.

Notice the new Munch Museum's leaning silhouette is deliberate — the top floors cantilever seaward, framing the fjord from inside.

Oslo Central Station (Oslo S)

Opened 1980 · site used since 1854

The current Oslo S opened in 1980, replacing the 1854 Christiania East station whose facade — the "Østbanehallen" — was preserved and now houses a food hall. The station is the terminus of the Bergensbanen, the Nordlandsbanen and the Dovrebanen, and one of the busiest railway hubs in Scandinavia.

Notice tomorrow's Bergensbanen departs from platforms 13–19 in the newer part of the station. The old 1854 facade is behind you as you walk out to the Opera.

Mathallen Oslo

Opened 2012 · Vulkan district · former iron foundry

The Vulkan Iron Foundry ran on the Akerselva river from 1873 until the 1950s, casting stoves and cookware. The buildings sat derelict for decades until a mid-2000s regeneration turned them into a mixed neighbourhood; Mathallen — the market hall — opened in 2012 in the largest of the surviving foundry sheds.

Speciality the district still runs a working hydro turbine on the Akerselva, tapping the fall the foundry once used for its bellows.

Fuglen

Coffee bar since 1963 · Tokyo branch since 2012

Fuglen — "the bird" — opened in 1963 as a Scandinavian design coffee bar and kept its 1960s interior intact through decades of neglect. In the 2000s it was reopened by a group who realised the untouched fittings were a heritage worth preserving. It now runs bars in Oslo, Tokyo and Asakusa, exporting the Norwegian mid-century interior back to the country that inspired it.

Notice every piece of furniture and lighting in the Oslo bar is period Scandinavian design and is for sale. The bar has doubled as a gallery of 1950s–60s Norwegian design since 2008.

On screen · Oslo

The Worst Person in the World (Verdens verste menneske)

2021 · Joachim Trier · Renate Reinsve · Cannes Best Actress

Almost every scene in Trier's Oslo trilogy finale is a place you can walk to. The bookshop, the café run, the Akerselva riverside, the run across the Grünerløkka bridge in the freeze-frame sequence — Oslo as its own restless character.

Occupied (Okkupert)

2015–2019 · Jo Nesbø · TV2

A near-future thriller in which Russia occupies Norway with the tacit approval of the EU. Filmed in Oslo, with Oslo S — where you'll board tomorrow's train — appearing in several key scenes.

Kon-Tiki

2012 · Rønning & Sandberg

The Oslo scenes were shot at Heyerdahl's real home and offices. The Fram Museum on Bygdøy — a short ferry ride from the Opera House — houses the actual Kon-Tiki raft.

Trollhunter (Trolljegeren)

2010 · André Øvredal

Cult-classic found-footage film about a state-employed troll hunter. Filmed all over Norway; the opening and closing sequences are in Oslo. Watch it once and you'll never stop looking at Norwegian rocks the same way.
