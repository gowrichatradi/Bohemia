Day 8 · Mon 5 Oct · Bardu / Abisko → Oslo

Back to Evenes, south to Oslo

A morning drive out of the Aurora country, back through Narvik on the E6/E10, and onto the 15:40 flight from Evenes. You land Oslo at 17:25 with a Monday evening open and one night before the Bergensbanen.

Did you knowThe Ofot Line from Kiruna to Narvik moves more freight tonnage than any other railway in Sweden — most of it iron ore heading for the ice-free Norwegian port. Norway got Narvik in 1905, Sweden built the line by 1902, and the entire town below is essentially the loading dock at the end of it.

Narvik & the iron-ore war

Ofot Line 1902 · Battles of Narvik April–June 1940

Narvik is Norway's only real ice-free port north of the Arctic Circle — the reason both sides fought so hard for it in April–June 1940. The town was fully rebuilt after the war and looks it: rectangular streets, mid-century concrete. What survived is the Ofot Line coming in from Sweden, still the main artery, still moving the ore. The Narvikfjellet cable car goes up the mountain behind the town in eight minutes and gives you the whole fjord.

Notice the war-memorial cemetery below the church — separate rows for Norwegian, Polish, French, British and German dead, all buried where they fell in the same three months of 1940.

Evenes Airport (Harstad-Narvik)

Opened 1972 · dual civilian-military

Evenes was built in 1972 to serve both Harstad and Narvik, which had refused for a decade to agree on a shared runway. The compromise site is halfway between them, on a Sámi reindeer-grazing plateau at 25 m above sea level — one of the few flat pieces of ground for 50 km. The military side now hosts Norwegian F-35s; you may see one taxiing while you queue for the Norwegian DY365.

Oslo · the newest old capital

Founded ~1049 · capital 1814 · Kristiania → Oslo 1925

Oslo has had three names — Ánslo in the Sagas, Christiania/Kristiania after Christian IV rebuilt it in 1624, and back to Oslo in 1925 by parliamentary vote. Copenhagen ruled it until 1814, Stockholm until 1905. This is the youngest continuously-independent capital in Europe, and it shows: the National Opera opened in 2008, the Munch Museum in 2021, the new National Museum in 2022. Every third building in the centre is a decade old.

Notice the National Opera roof — you walk up it, and you are on top of the opera house looking at the fjord. This was the first building of the modern rebuild.

Grünerløkka · the block across the river

Industrial 19th c · gentrified 1990s · Oslo's Berlin

The block across the Akerselva was Oslo's industrial working-class neighbourhood — textile mills, tanneries, the Freia chocolate factory (still there). It gentrified through the 1990s in the same wave that turned Berlin-Prenzlauerberg from squats into cafés. Now it is Oslo's Sunday-brunch neighbourhood: bookshops, coffee roasters, second-hand vinyl, and the Mathallen food market in a former iron foundry.

Munch & the National Museum

Munch Museum 2021 · National Museum 2022

Edvard Munch left his entire studio — 26,000 works — to the city of Oslo in 1944. For 76 years they were kept in a cramped building on the east side. In 2021 the new Munch Museum opened on the harbour, thirteen floors of one artist, and you can see the paint on The Scream up close in a room that was built for it. Sadly closed by 21:00 on Monday, but the shop and the ground floor stay open till 20:00.

Vigelandsparken

Sculpted 1924–1943 · 200+ figures · always open

Gustav Vigeland spent nineteen years carving 212 bronze and granite figures into one park on the west side of Oslo. The park is free, always open, floodlit at night, and the Monolith at its centre is a single seven-metre block of granite carved with 121 intertwined bodies. Nothing else in Europe is quite this obsessive; the Angry Boy (Sinnataggen) is the most-photographed bronze in Norway.

Told to the little one · The Ash-lad

Askeladden · Norwegian folk hero since forever

Every Norwegian child grows up with Askeladden (Ash-lad) — the youngest, laziest, ash-covered brother in every folktale who nevertheless outwits the trolls, marries the princess and inherits the kingdom. Not because he is strong or clever, but because he picks up small useless things nobody else bothers with, and they always turn out to matter. If you had to pick one Norwegian character for her to know, it is Askeladden. Every Norwegian bookshop has him in picture-book form.
