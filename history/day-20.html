Day 20 · Sat 17 Oct · Copenhagen — rest day

Back where you started

The final day. A hands-on science museum, a market that fixed Copenhagen's food scene, and the second-oldest amusement park on earth dressed for Halloween.

Did you knowTivoli's Boys Guard — children aged 9 to 16, in red uniforms and bearskins — has marched through the park since 1844. Selection is competitive and being a Tivoli guardsman is a real credit in a Copenhagen childhood. The Guard has its own brass band, cavalry section, and gilded royal coach that appear at ceremonial moments through the season, and it remains one of the oldest continuously operating boys' guard corps in the world.

Experimentarium

Founded 1991 · moved 2017 into a restored 1890s bottling plant · Hellerup

Copenhagen's science centre opened in 1991 in a former Tuborg brewery bottling plant. In 2017 it moved to a redesigned home on the same site — a heritage-listed 1890s industrial building given a copper-spiral facade by Denmark's CEBRA Architects. Three floors of hands-on exhibits, targeted at ages three to teenage, plus a giant Bubble Room and an outdoor rope playground overlooking the Sound.

Speciality the Experimentarium runs one of the most respected science-education outreach programmes in Europe, and its exhibits change every few months — so even locals return.

Den Blå Planet

Opened 2013 · Northern Europe's largest aquarium · architect 3XN

The Blue Planet aquarium opened in 2013 on a spit of land north of Kastrup airport, in a spiral building modelled on a whirlpool by Copenhagen architects 3XN. Its 53 aquaria hold 7 million litres of water and 20,000 animals across five ecosystems. The building sits on axis with the runway; the metro to Kastrup runs a stop past.

Notice the whirlpool plan is functional as well as visual — visitor flow follows the outer edge of the spiral inward, mimicking the descent from surface waters to the deep ocean.

Tivoli Gardens

Opened 15 August 1843 · world's third-oldest amusement park still operating · Walt Disney's inspiration

Tivoli was opened in 1843 by Georg Carstensen, who obtained a five-year permit from King Christian VIII with the argument that "when the people are amusing themselves, they do not think about politics". It is the world's third-oldest still-operating amusement park (after Bakken, also in Copenhagen, and one in the Netherlands). Walt Disney visited Tivoli several times in the 1950s and cited it as a direct inspiration for Disneyland — the layout, the parade routes, and the mix of curated fantasy with real gardens all trace back to Tivoli.

Speciality mid-October is Tivoli's Halloween season — 20,000 hand-carved pumpkins, dressed pathways, and a version of Halloween pitched at the youngest visitors. Open through 1 November.

Torvehallerne

Opened 2011 · Israels Plads · restored an open-air market that had run since the 19th c.

Torvehallerne opened in September 2011 on the site of the old Israels Plads open-air market, which had run for over a century but declined through the 1990s. The two glass halls hold about 80 stalls; Coffee Collective — the roastery that led Nordic light-roasting — has a bar inside. The market is credited with a full revival of Copenhagen's food scene.

Notice the Grød stall inside — the first porridge-only restaurant in Denmark — kicked off an unlikely revival of the Danish oat and rye porridge tradition, now common across the city.

Conditori La Glace

Opened 1870 · Denmark's oldest confectioner

La Glace has been on Skoubogade since 1870, run continuously by the Nimb and later Christensen families. Its layer cakes — particularly the Sportskage, invented in 1891 to mark a hit play, and the H.C. Andersen — are unchanged from 19th-century recipes. The white-uniformed staff and marble counters are original interior. It is one of the few surviving businesses on the street from before the great Copenhagen fires.

Notice the Sportskage — layers of choux, whipped cream and nougat — was named after Einar Christensen, a champion cyclist, and has been in continuous production for over 130 years.

Coffee Collective & the Nordic third wave

Founded 2007 · light-roasting movement

In 2007 four world-barista-champion-linked founders started Coffee Collective in Copenhagen with a commitment to direct-trade single-origin light-roasted coffee. Their shop and roastery in Frederiksberg — and later a bar inside Torvehallerne — became the international reference for "Nordic light roasting". Every Melbourne specialty roaster you know owes something to what these four figured out in Copenhagen 20 years ago.

Notice the shop next door to their roastery still runs the original manual pour-over bar with equipment that predates most modern specialty coffee tools.

Unique · Superkilen park, Nørrebro

A place most people miss

A linear park by BIG architects celebrating the 60+ nationalities of Nørrebro through actual objects — a Moroccan fountain, a Thai boxing ring, a neon octopus from Osaka. Free. Fifteen minutes on the metro from Kongens Nytorv.

Photo tip · Tivoli after 18:00 in October

The shot to actually take

Pumpkins lit, lantern paths, Nimb hotel facade in warm light. Bring a fast lens (f/1.8) for the low-light candy colours, and shoot at ISO 3200 to keep shutter speeds workable for the crowd movement.

For the little one

The three-year-old's version of today

Tivoli in Halloween dress — 20,000 pumpkins — is exactly right for her age. Get there before 17:00 for the earlier crowds; the peacock theatre and the wooden merry-go-round (opened 1900) both run continuously and don't need booking.

For the drive · This American Life — 'Return to the Scene of the Crime'

podcast · Ira Glass

One of TAL's best episodes on people revisiting places from their past — a fitting note for the day you close the trip in the city where you started. Under an hour.

On screen · Copenhagen, revisited

Wallander

IMDb 7.9 · 2005–2013 · Kenneth Branagh · BBC

Filmed in Ystad, across the Øresund Bridge in Sweden. But shots of the bridge crossing itself, and several Copenhagen interior scenes, appear throughout. Branagh's Wallander is the English version; the Swedish Krister Henriksson series (2005–2013) is closer to the Mankell novels.

Babette's Feast (Babettes gæstebud)

IMDb 7.7 · 1987 · Gabriel Axel · first Danish Oscar

The film that put Danish cinema on the modern map. Not set in Copenhagen — a remote Jutland fishing village — but the culinary sensibility (the rich French dinner at the heart of it) has fed straight into the Nordic cuisine you'll eat at Torvehallerne today.

Riget: Exodus

IMDb 7.4 · 2022 · Lars von Trier · DR

Von Trier revisited his 1994 hospital-horror The Kingdom, filming the sequel in the same Rigshospitalet building. Cannes premiered it out of competition. If you saw the trailer walking past the hospital yesterday, it wasn't your imagination.
