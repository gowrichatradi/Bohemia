Day 19 · Fri 16 Oct · Dresden → Berlin airport

Dresden — the whole day

The Frauenkirche is the emotional centre of the reconstructed city; the Neustadt across the river is the antidote. Both are worth an afternoon.

Did you knowThe golden cross on top of the Frauenkirche was made by Alan Smith — a British silversmith whose father, RAF Bomber Command, flew one of the missions that destroyed the church in February 1945. The cross was funded by donations from the UK "Dresden Trust", and Smith travelled to Dresden for the 2004 installation. The city, on the anniversary of the bombing, holds a candlelit human chain around the old town every year — locals and visitors, silent.

Frauenkirche

Built 1726–1743 · destroyed 1945 · rebuilt 1994–2005

The Frauenkirche was the greatest Protestant sandstone dome church in Europe when Bach played the organ at its 1736 inauguration. It survived the Seven Years' War, then collapsed on 15 February 1945 two days after the firebombing — the sandstone had absorbed enough heat to fail. The rubble was left as a memorial through the DDR period, and reconstruction began only after German reunification. It reopened in 2005, using 3,800 salvaged original stones set into new blocks — you can still read them on the facade as darker patches. Funding came from all over the world; the golden cross on top was made by a British silversmith whose father had flown a bomber over Dresden in 1945.

Notice the church is Protestant and holds no permanent artwork — everything you see, including Bach's copy of the Lutheran Bible on the altar, is a reconstruction of what stood before 1945.

The Zwinger

Built 1710–1728 · Matthäus Daniel Pöppelmann · Balthasar Permoser sculpture

August the Strong — Elector of Saxony and King of Poland — commissioned the Zwinger as a Baroque orangery, tournament ground and festival palace. It was originally intended as the forecourt of a much larger royal palace that was never built. The complex holds the Old Masters gallery (with Raphael's Sistine Madonna) and the Porcelain Collection (the largest in the world, based on August's obsession with Chinese and Meissen porcelain).

Speciality the Nymphenbad — the "Bath of the Nymphs" — is a small hidden Baroque courtyard tucked behind the pavilions, with a rocaille grotto and stone nymphs bathing under water jets. Rarely crowded.

Semperoper

First opera house 1841 · rebuilt after fire 1878 · rebuilt after 1945 bombing, reopened 1985

Gottfried Semper's opera house — Dresden's third — opened in 1841 and was one of the most celebrated Baroque Revival theatres in Europe. It burned down in 1869 and Semper rebuilt it (from exile in Vienna) in an even more elaborate form, reopened 1878. That building was destroyed in 1945. The DDR authorities rebuilt it almost stone for stone using pre-war drawings, and it reopened in 1985 — 40 years to the day after its destruction.

Notice Richard Wagner premiered Rienzi, The Flying Dutchman, and Tannhäuser at the Semperoper's predecessor. Richard Strauss premiered nine operas there. It is one of the most storied opera houses in Europe.

Bastei

Sandstone bridge 1851 · 194 m above the Elbe · Saxon Switzerland

The Bastei is a group of sandstone pillars rising 194 m above the Elbe in Saxon Switzerland, connected since 1851 by a stone bridge that crosses seven of them. A wooden bridge preceded it from 1824. The rock towers themselves have been climbing routes since the 1860s — Saxon climbers developed the rules of sport climbing here, and the Bastei area is where the "clean climbing" ethic began.

Notice viewing the Bastei from the rocks is free — a paid viewing platform sits just off it, but the stone bridge itself is a public path.

Kunsthofpassage (Hof der Elemente)

Created 1999 · Neustadt · musical funnels by Annette Paul, Christoph Roßner, André Tempel

In the late 1990s a group of local artists took over five linked courtyards in the Äußere Neustadt and turned each into a themed public art installation. The most famous is the "Hof der Elemente" — the Court of the Elements — whose facade is covered in funnels and gutters made of pale blue metal. When it rains, water runs through them and plays a rhythmic percussion.

Notice the courtyard next door — the Hof der Fabelwesen — has a wall of stylised mythical animals. The whole complex is free to wander at any hour.

Pfunds Molkerei

Opened 1880 · tiled by Villeroy & Boch · Guinness "most beautiful dairy shop"

Paul Gustav Leander Pfund opened the dairy in 1880. He commissioned Villeroy & Boch to tile the interior floor to ceiling with hand-painted majolica in a wildly ornate Neo-Renaissance style — 249 square metres of tile in the front shop alone. The dairy operated for over a century, was damaged in WWII, and reopened in its restored form in 1995. Guinness has listed it as the world's most beautiful dairy shop.

Speciality the shop still sells dairy — cheese, milk, condensed milk, hot chocolate — from the original 1880s counter. There is a small café upstairs.

For the little one

The three-year-old's version of today

The Kunsthofpassage's musical funnels in the Neustadt only play when it rains. If the day is dry, it's still a warren of interconnected courtyards to walk through and the murals on each are enough. Free, and next to a chocolate shop.

For the drive · Revolutions — 'The German Revolution 1848'

podcast · Mike Duncan

Duncan's series on the 1848 revolutions across Europe includes Saxony and Dresden explicitly. Sets up the last big cultural shift before the century of destruction that follows. Two hours.

On screen · Dresden & the Elbe

The Grand Budapest Hotel

IMDb 8.1 · 2014 · Wes Anderson

Görlitz, an hour up the Elbe. Anderson used the town's abandoned department store — Warenhaus Görlitz, built 1913 with a five-storey glass atrium — as the film's Grand Budapest lobby. It's now a museum and open to visit.

The Book Thief

IMDb 7.5 · 2013 · Brian Percival

Wartime Germany, filmed largely at Babelsberg Studios (Berlin) but with second-unit work in Görlitz and around Saxon Switzerland. The visual atmosphere — pale sandstone streetscapes, red-tile roofs — is exactly what Dresden looked like before 1945 and looks like again now.

Cloud Atlas

IMDb 7.4 · 2012 · Wachowskis & Tykwer

Filmed at Babelsberg (Berlin) with Dresden and Görlitz standing in for various fictional cities across the film's multiple timelines. Tom Hanks' 19th-century sequences use the reconstructed Neumarkt as backdrop.
