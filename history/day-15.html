Day 15 · Mon 12 Oct · Hallstatt → Český Krumlov → Prague

Traunsee, a walled town, and a river bend

The drive from Salzkammergut to Prague passes a Habsburg lake town, a medieval walled Austrian border town, and the second-most-visited UNESCO site in Czechia.

Gmunden & Schloss Ort

Salt trading town since 12th century · Schloss Ort founded c. 1080

Gmunden sits on the Traunsee and was the medieval endpoint of the Hallstatt salt trade — the "Salinen" barges brought salt down the Traun River to here before it was moved onward. Schloss Ort — the small island castle reached by a wooden footbridge — was founded around 1080 and is one of the oldest castles in Upper Austria. It was made famous by a 1990s Austrian television soap opera set on the island.

Notice Gmunden also gives its name to Gmundner Keramik — porcelain manufactured in the town since 1492, in continuous production for over 500 years. The green-striped pattern is the signature.

Freistadt

Founded ~1220 · walled medieval town on the salt road

Freistadt was founded in the 13th century by Duke Leopold VI to control the salt road between Bohemia and the Salzkammergut — the town's medieval walls, gates and moat are largely intact, making it one of the best-preserved walled towns in Austria. Its rectangular grid inside the walls is exactly what a planned 13th-century Bavarian foundation looked like.

Speciality the town has brewed its own beer — Freistädter Bier — since 1770, and the brewery is owned by the 149 houses inside the original walls: every "right holder" (Braukommune) inherits a share.

Český Krumlov

UNESCO 1992 · settlement 13th century · Rosenberg 1302–1602 · Eggenberg 1622–1719 · Schwarzenberg 1719–1947

Krumlov grew inside a tight horseshoe bend of the Vltava, dominated by the castle on the ridge above. The Rosenberg family made it the seat of one of the wealthiest Bohemian noble houses for three centuries; the town was locked in a time capsule when the estate passed through the Eggenbergs to the Schwarzenbergs, all of whom continued to add to the castle without demolishing the medieval structure. UNESCO listed it in 1992, immediately after the Velvet Revolution reopened Czechia to tourism.

Speciality the Baroque theatre inside the castle (1766) is one of only two in the world with its original stage machinery, costumes and lighting still functional. Performances still run in candlelight a few times a year.

The bears in the moat

Kept at Krumlov castle since 1707

Brown bears have been kept in the castle's dry moat since 1707, when the Eggenberg family — who then owned Krumlov — took them on as an heraldic reference to the Roman Orsini family, whose name means "little bears". The Rosenbergs and Eggenbergs both claimed distant Orsini kinship. The tradition has continued ever since; three bears live in the moat today.

Notice viewing is free — from the bridge into the castle — and the bears have their own "bear-keeper" position on the castle's staff, held continuously for 300 years.

The Cloak Bridge (Plášťový most)

Three-tiered stone bridge · 17th century · leads from castle to gardens

The Cloak Bridge is a three-arched viaduct connecting the castle courtyards to the Baroque gardens on the plateau — built in the 17th century as an elegant solution to the drop between the two levels. It replaced an earlier wooden covered walkway (the "cloak"). The bridge is one of the most photographed structures in the town, and its silhouette dominates every valley view.

Notice the bridge has three vertical levels — the top for foot access, the middle covered as a corridor, and the lowest for storage. Only the top is open to visitors.
