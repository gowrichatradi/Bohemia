Day 5 · Fri 2 Oct · Reine

The far west

Bridges, a preserved village, and a footpath onto a ridge. Everything today is on Flakstadøya and Moskenesøya, the two westernmost islands.

Did you knowThe Moskenes tidal current at the western tip of your island — the Moskstraumen — is the original "maelstrom", a Dutch loanword from Norse maalstrøm. It appears in Edgar Allan Poe, Jules Verne, and Herman Melville. On a spring tide the water can move at 20 knots — one of the strongest tidal currents in the world.

Reinebringen

448 m · Sherpa stone staircase completed 2019

Reinebringen was for years a dangerous scramble up a scree slope. Between 2016 and 2019 a team of Sherpa stonemasons from Nepal — the same craft tradition that maintains steps on Himalayan trails — laid 1,566 stone steps up the ridge, and the path is now a maintained trail. The view from the top back down over the Kirkefjord and the archipelago is the postcard of Lofoten.

Notice the stones are quarried locally, but the construction method — dry-set with keying stones — is Nepalese. It is one of the largest Sherpa-built trails outside the Himalayas.

The Fredvang bridges

Opened 1975 · Torvøy & Kubbelv bridges

The two white concrete arches connect Flakstadøya to the small island communities of Fredvang and Yttersand — before 1975 they were reached only by boat. The pair are among the earliest post-tensioned concrete bridges in northern Norway and were an experimental design at the time.

Notice the second bridge is a curve — most drivers focus on the straight first bridge and miss the more elegant one behind it.

Nusfjord

Preserved fishing village · UNESCO pilot conservation 1975

Nusfjord was one of the pilot sites for UNESCO's 1975 European Architectural Heritage Year — a rare intact 19th-century fishing village saved from decline. The general store from 1907, the cod-liver-oil factory and the boat-house are all still standing and functioning as a small museum/hotel ensemble. Fishing began here in medieval times.

Speciality the 1907 general store — Landhandleriet — is still a shop, and stocks the kind of goods (dry biscuits, tinned meat, coffee) a Lofoten fisherman would have bought in 1907.

Offersøykammen

436 m · Vestvågøy

A moderate ridge walk on Vestvågøy, less famous than Reinebringen and correspondingly quieter. It looks down on Nappstraumen, the narrow tidal strait between Flakstadøya and Vestvågøy, which was one of the busiest herring passages in Lofoten in the 19th century.

Notice from the top you see both major island groups laid out at once — Flakstad's mountains behind you, Vestvågøy's rolling farmland ahead. Almost nowhere else in Lofoten gives you both at once.

Nesland

Near-abandoned hamlet · reached on foot

Nesland was a working fishing settlement into the mid-20th century — a small church, a schoolhouse, a scatter of houses. It was largely abandoned as the fishery centralised on larger ports, and today only a handful of buildings remain, some in use as summer cabins. There are no facilities.

Speciality the tiny wooden church at Nesland, built 1888, is a rare survival — one of many small chapels of its era that once served every islet on this coast.

On screen · the far west

A Descent into the Maelström

Edgar Allan Poe · 1841 · short story

Not a film — but the reason to mention: Poe set his story of a Norwegian fisherman swept into a whirlpool at the exact tidal strait south of your window. Verne cribbed it for 20,000 Leagues; Melville put a version at the end of Moby-Dick. If you read one thing on the flight home, this is the one place on this trip that has its own literary genre.

Kon-Tiki

2012 · Joachim Rønning & Espen Sandberg

Nominated for Best Foreign Language Oscar. Filmed with sequences on the Norwegian coast and out at sea. Thor Heyerdahl grew up in Larvik far south, but the film's Norway scenes use the northern light.

Insomnia

1997 · Erik Skjoldbjærg · Norwegian original

The Nolan/Pacino remake was set in Alaska; the Norwegian original was filmed in Tromsø and the northern islands — including sequences that could have been shot on Flakstadøya. A detective in the arctic midnight sun, unable to sleep. The remake in 2002 was a straight adaptation.

North of the Sun (Nordfor Sola)

2012 · Inge Wegge · documentary

Two Norwegian surfers spend a winter in a driftwood hut on a remote Lofoten beach, surfing the arctic swell. Filmed near Unstad — the surf beach on your Day 6 route — and one of the best portraits of what Lofoten looks like in winter darkness.
