Day 4 · Thu 1 Oct · Reine

Reine, Sund, Å — the end of the road

Everything today happens between Reine and Å, along the last 25 km of European highway E10 before it stops at the sea.

Did you knowThe E10 you drove in on ends in the sea at Å — one of only three European highways whose numbering ends at a literal beach. The road-sign at the end has been stolen so many times that the road authority now bolts each replacement into the rock.

Å i Lofoten

Fishing village since medieval times · E10 terminus

Å — pronounced roughly "oh" — is the last village on Moskenesøya and the literal end of the E10, the European highway that runs from Å in Norway to Luleå in Sweden. It preserves an unusually intact fishing settlement: the Norwegian Fishing Village Museum here maintains twelve buildings — rorbuer, a cod-liver-oil press, the boathouse and forge — much as they were in the 1800s.

Speciality the tørrfisk drying racks along the shore. Stockfish — air-dried cod — has been exported from Lofoten to Italy and Portugal since at least the 12th century. The industry that built Reine is the industry you can smell here.

Bakeriet i Å

Building dated 1844 · wood-fired oven still in use

The bakery in Å has been in near-continuous use since 1844. The oven is the original wood-fired brick oven; the cinnamon buns and lefse come out of it every morning through the season. It is one of the oldest working bakeries in northern Norway.

Notice the building is part of the museum ensemble but keeps its commercial function — the bakery is not a reconstruction, just an old bakery that never stopped.

Sund Fiskerimuseum & the forge

Museum opened 1959 · forge working

The blacksmith Hans Gjertsen took over the forge in the 1970s and has since made a life's work of iron cormorants — the black seabird that hangs its wings out to dry on skerries all along this coast. Each is hammered on the anvil and sold from the workshop. Gjertsen's father ran the forge before him.

Notice the museum next door holds a collection of small Nordland fishing boats — the type that worked these waters under sail until the 1950s.

Sakrisøy

Painted yellow · Anita's Sjømat since 1970s

Sakrisøy is the odd one out — its rorbuer are painted ochre yellow, not red. The colour is a family tradition of the Steen family who have owned the islet for generations. Anita Marifjæren, who married in, has run the fish shop out of a former boat-house here since the 1970s, selling king crab, stockfish and salmon straight from the fjord.

Notice Olstinden — the sharp peak that rises directly behind Sakrisøy. Its shape is what makes the yellow-buildings-against-mountain frame the second-most-photographed view in Lofoten after Hamnøy.

Skagsanden beach

Flakstad · white sand at 68°N

Skagsanden is a shallow half-moon of white sand facing the Norwegian Sea. It has been a settled area since the Viking age — the medieval Flakstad church stands a kilometre inland, built in 1780 in the older stavkirke tradition. In winter the beach is a well-known aurora foreground, because it faces due north.

Speciality the sand itself is unusually pale for the latitude — most Arctic beaches are dark. Skagsanden's grains are quartz-rich glacial rock, ground light by ice.

On screen · around Reine

Ragnarok

IMDb 7.4 · 2020–2023 · Adam Price · Netflix

A modern-day retelling of Norse mythology with Thor as a teenager. Set in the (fictional) town of Edda, filmed largely in Odda on the Hardangerfjord (later on the trip), but the visual language — sheer mountain walls dropping into black water — is the exact Lofoten palette.

Frozen

IMDb 7.4 · 2013 · Chris Buck & Jennifer Lee · Disney

Not filmed here — but the concept team came to Norway in 2012 and Lofoten was part of the tour. The fishing rorbuer, mountain-and-water juxtaposition and the light on cold sea all fed into Arendelle. The kingdom's name is a nod to the Norwegian town of Arendal on the south coast.

Nord

IMDb 6.7 · 2009 · Rune Denstad Langlo · FIPRESCI Prize, Berlin

A quiet Norwegian road movie about a broken-down ski instructor who snowmobiles the length of the country. The Nordland leg passes through Lofoten scenery.
