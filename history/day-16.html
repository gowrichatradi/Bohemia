Day 16 · Tue 13 Oct · Prague — the old city

Prague — bridge, castle, tower

Nearly everything on today's map was here before Australia was mapped. The bridge is 1357; the castle is 9th century; the astronomical clock has kept working for six centuries.

Did you knowThe mortar used to build Charles Bridge was mixed with eggs — thousands of them — supposedly gathered from every village in Bohemia. One town sent its eggs hard-boiled, either as a joke or from misunderstanding. The recipe has been chemically confirmed: modern restoration analysis in the 2000s found egg proteins bonded into the original 14th-century stonework.

Charles Bridge

Foundation stone laid 1357 · Emperor Charles IV · Peter Parler

Charles IV — Holy Roman Emperor and King of Bohemia — laid the foundation stone at 5:31 a.m. on 9 July 1357: a palindromic number (1-3-5-7-9-7-5-3-1) chosen by his astronomers for maximum astrological strength. The bridge was built by Peter Parler, the same architect responsible for St Vitus Cathedral, and took nearly fifty years. For 400 years it was the only bridge across the Vltava in Prague. The 30 Baroque statues along it are 17th-century additions; most are now replicas, the originals housed in the Lapidárium.

Notice the statue of St. John of Nepomuk (1683) — the fifth from the Old Town side, right — has a bronze plaque below it worn shiny by centuries of touch for luck. He was thrown from the bridge by Wenceslas IV in 1393.

Prague Castle

Founded c. 880 CE · largest ancient castle complex in the world (Guinness)

Prague Castle has been a seat of royal, imperial and presidential power for over a thousand years — the residence of the Přemyslid dukes, the Bohemian kings, Holy Roman Emperor Charles IV, the Habsburgs, and today the Czech president. At 570 metres long and 130 wide, Guinness lists it as the largest ancient castle in the world. Its buildings span every European architectural period from Romanesque onward.

Notice the changing of the guard at noon includes a fanfare from the palace windows above — the only guard-change in central Europe with its own composed music.

St Vitus Cathedral

Begun 1344 · completed 1929 · Peter Parler & Matthias of Arras

St Vitus took nearly six centuries to finish. Matthias of Arras began the eastern end in 1344; Peter Parler continued it after 1352 with radical Gothic vaulting that anticipated late-medieval building by a century. The nave was left roofless in the Hussite wars, and the western half was finally built in the neo-Gothic style between 1873 and 1929. The Alfons Mucha window in the north wall — Art Nouveau, 1931 — is unlike anything else in a medieval European cathedral.

Speciality Wenceslas Chapel — the shrine of Bohemia's patron saint, murdered by his brother in 935 — has walls set with 1,300 semi-precious stones. Its door is locked with seven keys, held by seven different officials.

The Astronomical Clock (Orloj)

Installed 1410 · oldest working astronomical clock in the world

The Orloj on the Old Town Hall was built in 1410 by Mikuláš of Kadaň, with the astronomical dial designed by the astronomer Jan Šindel. The calendar dial was added in the late 15th century; the moving apostles were installed in the 17th. It has been repaired many times — most recently after the Old Town Hall was set on fire by SS troops in May 1945 — but the mechanism at its heart is the original 15th-century one. It is the third-oldest astronomical clock ever made and the oldest still working.

Speciality the clock does not just tell time — it tells four different times at once (Old Bohemian, German, Babylonian and sidereal) plus the position of the sun and moon in the zodiac.

Petřín Hill lookout tower

Built 1891 · 63.5 m · 1:5 scale echo of the Eiffel Tower

The Petřín Tower was built in 1891 for the Prague Jubilee Exhibition, two years after the Eiffel Tower in Paris — Czech mountaineers had visited the Paris Exposition and returned determined that Prague should have its own. It is roughly one-fifth the height of the Eiffel and stands on the crest of Petřín Hill, so the top of the tower sits at about the same absolute altitude as the top of the Eiffel Tower's second platform. It has been in continuous use since 1891.

Notice the funicular that carries you up Petřín Hill (opened 1891 for the same exhibition) takes standard Prague transit tickets.

Strahov Library

Theological Hall 1671 · Philosophical Hall 1794 · Premonstratensian monastery

The Strahov Monastery library holds two of the most beautiful Baroque interiors in Europe. The Theological Hall (1671) has a low, painted ceiling by Siard Nosecký; the Philosophical Hall (1794) is a soaring room with a ceiling fresco by the Viennese painter Franz Anton Maulbertsch. Between them the library holds over 200,000 volumes, some manuscripts from the 9th century. It has been in continuous use as a monastic library for over 350 years.

Notice the "cabinet of curiosities" corridor between the two halls contains, among other oddities, a preserved dodo and a series of skulls used by the monks for anatomy study.

Kubista & the House of the Black Madonna

Built 1912 · architect Josef Gočár · world's only Cubist building

Czech Cubism was a short-lived movement (roughly 1910–1914) that tried to apply Cubist principles from painting to architecture, furniture and decorative art. The House of the Black Madonna — with its faceted stone facade, angular window bays and Cubist wrought-iron work — is the movement's masterwork. The name comes from a 17th-century statue of a black Madonna set into the corner from an earlier house on the site.

Speciality Kubista, the shop inside, sells reproductions of Cubist chairs, cups and lamps. Nowhere else on earth manufactures functional Cubist furniture.

Havelské tržiště

Trading since 1232 · oldest open-air market in Prague

The Havel market has traded on the same lane since it was founded by Wenceslas I in 1232 as one of several planned markets in the medieval Old Town. Originally a general goods market, it now runs mostly as a fruit-and-craft market by day. The building line and orientation of the stalls has not changed in almost 800 years.

Notice the church of St Havel (Havelský kostel) on the north side — one of the earliest Gothic hall churches in Bohemia, from around 1265.

Café Louvre & Kavárna Slavia

Louvre opened 1902 · Slavia opened 1884

Kavárna Slavia has been open on the Vltava embankment since 1884, and was the daily haunt of Rilke, Franz Kafka and — during the communist decades — the Charter 77 dissidents including Václav Havel. Café Louvre opened in 1902 in a first-floor room on Národní; Albert Einstein was a regular during his time at Prague's Charles-Ferdinand University (1911–12), and Karel Čapek's Friday-night writers' circle met there through the 1920s.

Notice Louvre's rear billiard room — still operating with its 1902 tables — is the only pre-war café billiard room in the city that has stayed continuously in use.

Nový Svět

"New World" · cottages 16th–17th century · behind Prague Castle

A lane of small painted cottages behind the castle, built in the 16th century to house castle staff. The astronomers Tycho Brahe (from 1599) and Johannes Kepler (who worked with him and succeeded him) both lived in houses here — Kepler at No. 76, Brahe at No. 1. Nový Svět was the "poor quarter" of Hradčany then; today it is one of the quietest lanes in the whole city.

Notice the golden pear on the sign of the "U Zlaté hrušky" restaurant on the corner — a common Prague signage tradition where the house was named for its emblem, not its number, before street numbering arrived in the 18th century.

Golden Lane (Zlatá ulička)

16th century cottages · inside Prague Castle

A tiny lane of 11 pastel cottages built into the north wall of Prague Castle. Originally 16th-century housing for the castle marksmen; later occupied by goldsmiths (from which the name comes, though the 'golden' claim is disputed). In 1917 Franz Kafka rented Nr. 22 — a blue cottage the size of a garden shed — where he wrote much of A Country Doctor in the winter of that year.

Notice included in the standard Prague Castle ticket. Go before 09:00 or after 16:00 — coach tours pack the lane between. Some cottages are set-dressed as period rooms (goldsmith, herbalist, seamstress); Kafka's Nr. 22 is now a tiny bookshop.

Franz Kafka (1883–1924)

Prague's most famous writer · lived in Golden Lane

Born to a German-speaking Jewish family on the edge of the Old Town Square. Wrote The Trial, The Castle and The Metamorphosis, mostly in German, mostly at night — he worked as an insurance clerk at the Workers' Accident Insurance Institute on Na Poříčí by day. He rented Golden Lane Nr. 22 in 1917 to write in peace. Died of tuberculosis at 40 in 1924, leaving instructions to his friend Max Brod to burn all his unpublished work — Brod famously ignored him and the world got most of the Kafka canon.

Notice Kafka is buried in the New Jewish Cemetery in Žižkov, a 20-min tram east. His childhood home is on the northwest corner of the Old Town Square — plaque on the wall.

Unique · Vojan Gardens, Kampa Island

A place most people miss

A walled Baroque garden three minutes off Charles Bridge, with resident peacocks and a Baroque chapel almost nobody knows exists. Free entry. Locals eat lunch here in October when tourists are pointing at the bridge.

Photo tip · Charles Bridge at 05:30

The shot to actually take

Empty, blue hour on the Vltava, statues silhouetted. Shoot from the Old Town tower end looking west toward the castle — the tower gives you an elevated foreground and the bridge falls away toward the illuminated castle behind.

For the little one

The three-year-old's version of today

The Petřín funicular ride, the mirror maze, and the lookout tower — a small Eiffel Tower — are the perfect Prague afternoon for her age. The funicular takes standard Prague transit tickets, so no separate booking. Ninety minutes total, unhurried.

For the drive · The Rest Is History — 'Charles IV and Golden Prague'

podcast · Holland & Sandbrook

The story of the Holy Roman Emperor who built Prague as you see it. Charles Bridge, St. Vitus, the Astronomical Clock — all his projects. Two hours; brilliant background before the day.

On screen · Prague — the world's cheapest Vienna

Amadeus

IMDb 8.4 · 1984 · Miloš Forman · 8 Oscars

The film that convinced Hollywood you could shoot 18th-century Vienna in Prague. Forman filmed at the Estates Theatre (where Mozart himself conducted the premiere of Don Giovanni in 1787), Nový Svět, Golden Lane and Loreto. The candlelit Prague you'll walk tonight is his Prague.

Casino Royale

IMDb 8.0 · 2006 · Martin Campbell · Daniel Craig

Bond's first mission — the Montenegro casino was actually Karlovy Vary and the Grandhotel Pupp; the Venice climax used Prague interiors including Barrandov Studios. Bond's introduction ('The name's Bond') happens in a Prague-shot bathroom.

The Bourne Identity

IMDb 7.8 · 2002 · Doug Liman · Matt Damon

Bourne's 'Zurich' apartment sequence, the safehouse and much of Europe were shot in Prague — a technique that half the Hollywood action franchise then copied. The Metro stations you'll use tomorrow have starred as Zurich, Moscow, Berlin and Paris.
