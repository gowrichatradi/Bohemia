Day 17 · Wed 14 Oct · Prague — the third day

Vyšehrad, Kutná Hora, or the zoo

The day the third night bought — either Prague's mythic origin fortress and a silver-boom UNESCO town, or a world-class zoo reached by riverboat.

Did you knowPrague Zoo lost 134 animals in the 2002 flood. One sea lion, Gaston, escaped his enclosure and swam down the Vltava into the Elbe — he made it around 300 km through Germany before being caught near Dresden, exhausted, and died a week later. Gaston is now a symbol of the rebuilt zoo, and Czech schoolchildren learn his story as a lesson about the flood.

Vyšehrad

Fortress founded 10th c. · Basilica of St Peter & Paul rebuilt 1885–1903

According to legend Vyšehrad is where Princess Libuše, mythic mother of the Czechs, prophesied a great city — Prague — from its ramparts. Archaeologically it is one of Prague's oldest sites, occupied as a royal seat before Prague Castle itself in the 11th century. Its current character is 19th-century: the neo-Gothic Basilica of St Peter and Paul (1885–1903), the ramparts rebuilt as a public park, and the Slavín cemetery inside them where Antonín Dvořák, Bedřich Smetana, Alfons Mucha and Karel Čapek are all buried.

Speciality the rotunda of St Martin inside the fortress — 11th century — is the oldest still-standing rotunda in Prague.

Kutná Hora

UNESCO 1995 · silver boom 13th–16th c. · Bohemian royal mint

Kutná Hora was Bohemia's silver capital. Silver was discovered here in the 1290s, and for two centuries the town's mines produced enough silver to make its Prague Groschen the standard European currency — Kutná Hora was second in wealth only to Prague itself in the Bohemian kingdom. When the silver ran out in the 16th century the town froze in time, which is why its Gothic and Renaissance architecture is so intact today.

Notice the Italian Court (Vlašský dvůr) was the royal mint from 1300, where Florentine minters were brought in to strike the Prague Groschen. The vault where the coins were struck is still visible.

St. Barbara's Cathedral, Kutná Hora

Begun 1388 · finished 1905 · patron saint of miners

St. Barbara — patron saint of miners because she is invoked against sudden death — got Kutná Hora's greatest church, funded by mining-guild money. Peter Parler's workshop began it in 1388; the cathedral was built in fits and starts for over 500 years, and only fully completed in 1905. Its flying buttresses and net-vaulted interior are among the finest late-Gothic in Central Europe, and the 15th-century frescoes inside depict miners and mint workers at their jobs — a rare non-religious subject on a cathedral wall.

Speciality the frescoes of miners at work are unique in Gothic cathedral art. Nowhere else does a medieval church put the local labourers on its walls as the subject.

Sedlec Ossuary

Founded 13th c. · bones arranged 1870 by František Rint

The chapel below the Cistercian church at Sedlec became a burial ground of exceptional prestige after a monk returned from Jerusalem in 1278 carrying earth from Golgotha, which he scattered in the cemetery. Being buried there became so desirable that during the 14th-century plague and the 15th-century Hussite wars, some 40,000 people were interred — far more than the ground could hold. In 1870 a woodcarver called František Rint was hired to bring order to the accumulated bones; he arranged them into a chandelier, a coat of arms of the Schwarzenberg family, and pyramids in the corners.

Notice the Schwarzenberg coat of arms on the wall — a raven pecking the eyes of a Turkish head — is made entirely of human bone, including finger bones for the birds' claws.

Prague Zoo

Opened 1931 · rebuilt after 2002 flood · consistently ranked in world top 10

Prague Zoo opened in 1931 on a hillside in Troja, north of the city. It was catastrophically flooded in the great Vltava flood of August 2002, when water reached ten metres above normal — 130 animals died and much of the zoo was destroyed. The reconstruction is a modern zoo of exceptional design; it consistently ranks in world top-10 lists. The chairlift over the enclosures dates from that rebuild.

Speciality Prague Zoo runs the world's leading breeding programme for Przewalski's horses — the last truly wild horse — and has reintroduced dozens to the Mongolian steppe.

Jiřího z Poděbrad market & the Church of the Sacred Heart

Church 1929–1932 · architect Jože Plečnik

The farmers' market runs Wednesday to Saturday on the square below one of the great 20th-century churches of Europe — Jože Plečnik's Church of the Most Sacred Heart of Our Lord, completed 1932. Plečnik — best known for reshaping Ljubljana — was a Slovenian architect who spent years redesigning parts of Prague Castle for the Czechoslovak state; this church, with its enormous glazed clock tower and unusual dark-brick facade, is his signature work.

Notice the clock tower is walkable inside — the ramp goes right up behind the enormous glass face, where you can look at the city through the reversed numerals.

Unique · Wallenstein Garden, Malá Strana

A place most people miss

The private Baroque garden of the 17th-century Wallenstein Palace (now the Czech Senate). Sala terrena, artificial dripstone cave, resident peacocks, and a formal parterre lined with bronze copies of statues Swedish troops took to Stockholm in 1648. Free.

Photo tip · Vyšehrad at dusk

The shot to actually take

The neo-Gothic basilica's twin spires against the sunset — best from the ramparts on the west side, with the Vltava 100 metres below you. Bring a tripod; twilight is short in October.

For the little one

The three-year-old's version of today

Prague Zoo is world-top-ten and the chairlift over the enclosures is what she'll remember. The Vltava boat up to it takes 75 minutes each way and is half the fun; check it's still running in mid-October before committing the morning.

For the drive · 99% Invisible — 'Sedlec Ossuary'

podcast · Roman Mars

The show has a specific episode on the bone-decorated chapel at Sedlec — the exact one you'll visit in Kutná Hora. Under 30 minutes; ideal for the train ride out.

On screen · Kutná Hora & Prague on film

Snowpiercer

IMDb 7.0 · 2013 · Bong Joon-ho

Almost entirely shot at Barrandov Studios in Prague — the train's interior sets were built end-to-end in the studio's biggest sound stage. If you visited Prague Zoo today, you passed Barrandov on the way back.

Kafka

IMDb 6.8 · 1991 · Steven Soderbergh · Jeremy Irons

Filmed almost entirely in Prague, treating Kafka's own city as a character. Cemeteries and the castle appear. Rarely shown now but influential — the black-and-white Prague of this film shaped how the city was photographed for years.

Blade II

IMDb 6.7 · 2002 · Guillermo del Toro · Wesley Snipes

Filmed in Prague. Del Toro used Vyšehrad's cemetery for one of the vampire hive sequences — the ramparts and the neo-Gothic basilica are unmistakable if you look for them.
