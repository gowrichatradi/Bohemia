Day 14 · Sun 11 Oct · Geroldsee → the Salzkammergut

Karwendel, gorges, and salt

From an alpine hay meadow to a village mining salt for 7,000 years — one of the oldest continuously worked industrial sites on earth.

Did you knowHallstatt has been so exactly replicated that a full-scale copy of the village — church, houses, lake shore — was built in Guangdong Province, China, and opened in 2012. It cost $940 million. Hallstatt's mayor was not told beforehand and only found out when Chinese tourists began arriving asking whether the "original" was faithful to the copy.

Geroldsee (Wagenbrüchsee)

Small alpine lake · Almhütten in the meadow

The lake sits at 754 m on a hay meadow at the foot of the Karwendel massif. The wooden Almhütten — hay barns — that stand in the field are traditional Upper Bavarian farm architecture, some over a century old. The setting was a working meadow long before it became one of the most-photographed sunrise spots in Bavaria.

Speciality the Almhütten are still used by local farmers to store hay. They are not stage dressing.

Partnachklamm

Opened to the public 1912 · 700 m long · 80 m deep

The Partnach Gorge was cut by the meltwater of the Schneeferner glacier over thousands of years. It was made passable by loggers who blasted a walkway through the rock in 1912 so timber could be floated out to Garmisch — the same path visitors now walk. Loggers used the gorge into the 1960s. It is one of very few gorges in Bavaria open year-round.

Notice in winter the interior freezes into an ice cathedral. The walk-in path is cut behind waterfalls, so bring waterproofs.

Eibsee

Under the Zugspitze · 973 m altitude · glacial origin

Eibsee is a private lake — owned by the Schön family since the 19th century — beneath Germany's highest mountain, the Zugspitze (2,962 m). Its turquoise colour comes from suspended glacial rock flour. The Bayerische Zugspitzbahn cable car station on its shore was originally built in 1963, and updated in 2017 with a car holding 120 people that broke three world records.

Notice the eight small islands in the lake are called the Eibsee-Inseln; they were formed by a rockslide in the 15th or 16th century when part of the Zugspitze wall collapsed.

Ramsau bei Berchtesgaden

Parish church of St. Sebastian consecrated 1512

The Alpine church of St. Sebastian — with its onion-domed white tower and the Watzmann massif behind — is one of the most-photographed churches in Germany. The current building dates from 1512. The village around it grew as a pilgrimage stop and later as a base for Alpine climbers heading up the Reiter Alm.

Speciality the Hintersee and Zauberwald — the "Magic Forest" — behind Ramsau is a subalpine woodland of moss-covered boulders and ancient trees, protected as a nature reserve since 1949. It inspired several 19th-century landscape painters of the Ramsau school.

Hallstatt

UNESCO 1997 · salt mining since ~5000 BCE · Hallstatt Culture named for the site

The Hallstatt salt mine is arguably the oldest continuously worked mine in the world — wooden mining tools found here have been carbon-dated to the Neolithic. The "Hallstatt Culture" (roughly 1200–500 BCE) is named for the burial ground excavated here in the 1840s, which produced 1,300 graves and defined an entire archaeological era of European prehistory. UNESCO listed the whole Hallstatt–Dachstein–Salzkammergut region in 1997.

Speciality the salt mine is still commercially productive — Salinen Austria pumps brine out through the same shafts. The Salzwelten tourist mine and the Rudolfstower Skywalk above sit directly above the working operation.

The Hallstatt Beinhaus (Ossuary)

In the parish charnel chapel of St. Michael · practice began 12th century

Because the churchyard is small and hemmed in by the mountain, Hallstatt families adopted the practice of exhuming skulls after 10–15 years, painting them with the name of the deceased, their date of death, and floral wreaths, and stacking them in the Beinhaus. About 1,200 skulls survive. The practice slowed in the 20th century but the most recent painted skull dates from 1995.

Notice each skull is legible — names, dates and family emblems — and specific plants around the temples denote relationships. It is one of very few charnel houses in Europe where the collection is legible as individuals rather than anonymous remains.

Bad Ischl & Konditorei Zauner

Imperial spa town · Zauner founded 1832

Bad Ischl was the summer capital of the Habsburg emperors from around 1830. Emperor Franz Joseph signed the Austrian declaration of war on Serbia here in July 1914, formally starting the First World War. Konditorei Zauner opened in 1832 as court confectioner to the imperial family; Zauner-Stollen — an almond-and-nougat pastry — was invented here and is still made in the same rooms.

Notice the emperor's summer villa (Kaiservilla) at Bad Ischl is still owned by the Habsburg family and open as a museum. Franz Joseph signed his war declaration in a room now called the "war salon".

For the little one

The three-year-old's version of today

The Hallstatt funicular up to the Skywalk platform is the kid-friendly version of the salt mine (which bars under-4s). She'll get the view, the ride and the coffee at the top; the mine below is barred to her anyway. Choose Eibsee earlier if the weather holds.

For the drive · The Rest Is History — Franz Joseph & Sisi

podcast · Holland & Sandbrook

Their two-parter on the Habsburgs is very good, and Bad Ischl — where you'll stop for coffee at Zauner — is where much of it happened. Roughly two hours total.

On screen · the Salzkammergut

The Sound of Music

IMDb 8.1 · 1965 · Robert Wise · Julie Andrews

The Salzkammergut is the Sound of Music landscape. St. Wolfgang and Mondsee (the wedding church) are both on today's route. The 'Do Re Mi' scenes are around Mirabell in Salzburg (an hour north-west) and on the Werfen meadow east of Salzburg.

The Empress (Die Kaiserin)

IMDb 7.7 · 2022 · Netflix

A modern retelling of the Empress Elisabeth story, filmed in Bavaria and Austria. Bad Ischl and the imperial villa there appear as themselves. The palace scenes are Schloss Schönbrunn in Vienna.

Woman in Gold

IMDb 7.3 · 2015 · Simon Curtis · Helen Mirren

The story of Maria Altmann's fight to recover Klimt's Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I. Vienna-set but with sequences around Aussee and the Salzkammergut where the Bloch-Bauer family had summer homes. The salt mines you're standing above were among the places Nazi loot was hidden during WWII.
