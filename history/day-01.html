Day 1 · Mon 28 Sep · Melbourne → Copenhagen

Copenhagen — a half day, on foot

Land, drop bags, keep moving. Every place you pass today is walkable from the centre, and every one of them was already old when Melbourne was a paddock.

Did you knowCopenhagen's harbour water is clean enough to swim in — the city started allowing it in 2002, and there are now four public harbour baths inside the centre. It is one of very few capital cities in the world where you can jump straight off the wharf.

Kastrup Airport & the M2

Opened 1925 · Metro line M2 opened 2007

Copenhagen Airport is one of the oldest still in continuous civilian use anywhere — the grass strip at Kastrup began taking regular flights in 1925. The M2 metro line reached Terminal 3 in 2007 and is one of the few fully driverless airport metros in Europe.

Notice the metro carriages have no driver cabin — walk to the front for the tunnel view. It runs every 4–6 minutes to Kongens Nytorv.

Kongens Nytorv

Laid out 1670 · Baroque royal square

Christian V laid out the square in 1670, ringed by townhouses for nobles who had to fund construction themselves in exchange for the address. The equestrian statue at the centre is Christian V as a Roman emperor — the oldest royal equestrian statue in Scandinavia.

Notice the Charlottenborg palace on the east side — since 1754 it has housed the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts, one of the oldest art schools in Europe.

Nyhavn

Canal dug 1670–1673 · painted 17th–18th c. townhouses

Nyhavn — literally "new harbour" — was dug by Swedish prisoners of war to bring seagoing trade right up to Kongens Nytorv. For two centuries it was a rough sailors' district. Hans Christian Andersen lived at three different addresses on the canal — Nr. 20 (1834), Nr. 67 (1848–1865), and Nr. 18 (1871–1875), where he wrote much of the fairy-tale canon.

Speciality the north side (odd numbers) catches the sun and is where the tourist restaurants sit; the south side (even numbers) has the older, taller buildings and is where locals still walk. Number 9 dates from 1681 — the oldest on the canal.

Kongens Have & Rosenborg

Garden laid out 1606 · castle built 1606–1633

The King's Garden is the oldest royal garden in Denmark, established by Christian IV around his new summer palace, Rosenborg. Christian IV built the castle in Dutch Renaissance style over nearly three decades, and it kept the Danish Crown Jewels once his descendants moved on to Christiansborg.

For the little one the free marionette theatre — Marionetteatret — runs afternoon shows in the park on autumn weekends, a tradition going back to 1949. And the Royal Guard's changing march passes through the park each day at 11:30.

Ofelia Plads

Opened 2015 · former harbour warehouse district

The whole stretch north of Nyhavn was working freight docks until the early 2000s, when the container trade left. Ofelia Plads is what the city put on top — a public deck reaching out over the harbour beside the Royal Playhouse (2008, Lundgaard & Tranberg).

Notice the Opera House across the water — built 2001–2004 as a gift from A.P. Møller. It sits on axis with Amalienborg and Frederiks Kirke, so the sightline you get from Ofelia Plads is a manufactured 21st-century one.

For the little one

The three-year-old's version of today

The Copenhagen changing of the guard passes through the King's Garden at 11:30 every day — free and short enough for a three-year-old. If she has the energy, the Little Mermaid is a fifteen-minute walk down the harbour, and smaller than expected in a way children find funny.

For the drive · The Rest Is History

podcast · Tom Holland & Dominic Sandbrook

Search their Viking or Hans Christian Andersen episodes — the pair make Danish history sound like gossip. Two hours in the airport queue passes fast.

On screen · Copenhagen

Borgen

IMDb 8.4 · 2010–2013, 2022 · Sidse Babett Knudsen · DR

Christiansborg — the parliament and prime-minister's office, five minutes south of Nyhavn — is the Borgen of the title. External shots were filmed on Slotsholmen; the interiors were built on a soundstage but the corridors are exact copies.

The Bridge (Bron/Broen)

IMDb 8.6 · 2011–2018 · Sofia Helin, Kim Bodnia · SVT/DR

Opens with a body found on the exact centre line of the Øresund Bridge — the road-rail crossing you flew over on approach. Copenhagen scenes swap between the docks, Nørrebro's rougher edge, and the airport itself.

The Killing (Forbrydelsen)

IMDb 8.4 · 2007–2012 · Sofie Gråbøl · DR

The show that started the Nordic-noir wave. Sarah Lund's Faroese jumper became a cultural phenomenon, and much of the exterior work was shot around Vesterbro, the harbour and the Politigården police headquarters — the 1924 stone monolith on Polititorvet that's a five-minute walk from your hotel.
