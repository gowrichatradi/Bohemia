Day 3 · Wed 30 Sep · Denmark → Lofoten

The road west to Reine

Copenhagen to Evenes to the Lofoten wall. The places you pass on this drive are the ones that made Norway rich before oil — the cod trade, unchanged in outline for eight hundred years.

Did you knowThe stockfish drying on the racks you'll see all around Reine and Hamnøy has been Italy's Christmas Eve dish for centuries — Bacalà alla Vicentina is made from Lofoten cod, and Italy is still the single biggest export market for Norwegian tørrfisk, a trade line that has run continuously since about 1100.

Evenes / Harstad-Narvik Airport

Opened 1973 · former NATO air base

Evenes was built as a Cold War fighter base and civilianised in 1973 to serve both Harstad and Narvik. It still hosts a Norwegian Air Force detachment — you may see F-35s on the apron alongside the SAS 737 you flew in on. The airport is the gateway to Lofoten for anyone not taking the coastal ferry.

Notice from the drive west, the airport sits on the edge of the Ofoten fjord — the same fjord that saw two naval battles in April 1940 during the German invasion of Norway.

Lofast (the E10 bridges)

Completed 2007 · fixed link to the mainland

Until 2007 Lofoten was reached only by ferry. The Lofast route — Lofotens fastlandsforbindelse — strings the E10 across a chain of bridges and tunnels from the mainland through Hinnøya to Austvågøy, and it is the only reason today's drive is possible in one afternoon. Before Lofast the same trip meant an overnight on the Hurtigruten coastal steamer.

Notice the Raftsund and the Trollfjord open off the E10 near Digermulen — the narrow branch fjord where the Hurtigruten still swings its bow in summer. You cross the mouth of it at speed and probably do not notice.

Sildpollnes church

Built 1891 · Austnesfjorden

The small wooden church sits on a spit reaching into Austnesfjorden. It was built in 1891 to serve the fishing communities on the fjord, and the setting — mountains on three sides, water on all — has made it one of the most photographed churches in northern Norway. It is still an active parish church.

Speciality the spit is the point. Photographers stop for the church-plus-peaks composition — the Trolltindan massif behind — that only exists because someone in 1891 chose this narrow tongue of land for the pulpit.

Svolvær

Founded as a fishing station 1500s · town rights 1918

Svolvær grew as a base for the Lofotfisket, the winter cod fishery that has drawn fleets from all over Norway since at least the 12th century. In February and March, the population historically doubled with visiting fishermen. The town got its rights in 1918 and is now the administrative capital of Lofoten.

Notice Svolværgeita — "the Goat" — the twin-horned rock pinnacle above the town. Free-climbers jump between its two horns; you can see them from the harbour on clear days.

Reine & Hamnøy

Fishing settlement since the 1400s · rorbu tradition

Hamnøy is claimed as the oldest fishing village in Lofoten, mentioned in records from the 16th century. Reine was a base for the same Lofotfisket that fed Svolvær, and the red-painted rorbuer — the fishermen's cabins on stilts over the water — were built by boat-owners to lodge crews arriving each winter from the coast further south. Most of what you sleep in tonight is a converted or reconstructed rorbu.

Speciality the red paint. It is Falu red — iron-oxide pigment from copper mining in Falun, Sweden, used since the 16th century because it was the cheap paint that fishermen could actually afford. Almost every rorbu in Lofoten is that colour for that reason.

Unique · The Trollfjord

A place most people miss

A two-kilometre branch fjord too narrow for most vessels, where the Hurtigruten coastal steamer traditionally swings its bow in as a demonstration of tight-turn seamanship. Day-tour boats run from Svolvær through October — worth chasing if the sea is calm.

Photo tip · Sildpollnes church

The shot to actually take

Walk 100 metres off the roadside pullout to the tip of the spit. The church-plus-Trolltindan-peaks composition is at its best in the last hour of light — the church face lit warm, the mountains cool behind.

For the little one

The three-year-old's version of today

The 4h45 drive west across Lofoten has one guaranteed stop that saves the day: Svolvær harbour. There are painted wooden fishing boats to touch, a proper harbour to circle on foot, and a bakery three minutes from the marina. Forty-five minutes there and everyone gets back in the car willingly.

For the drive · The Vinland Sagas — audio adaptation

audiobook · trans. Keneva Kunz

The two short Norse sagas about Leif Erikson's voyages to America. Under three hours narrated. Perfect for the drive north — you're literally following his coastline.

On screen · Lofoten & the arctic north

Kon-Tiki

IMDb 7.2 · 2012 · Rønning & Sandberg · Oscar-nominated

The story of Thor Heyerdahl's 1947 raft voyage. Norwegian sequences use the northern coastline; a landmark of modern Norwegian cinema.

Kampen om Narvik (Narvik: Hitler's First Defeat)

IMDb 6.6 · 2022 · Erik Skjoldbjærg · Netflix

Filmed in Narvik itself and on the Ofoten fjord — the same fjord your Evenes flight lands beside. The April 1940 Battle of Narvik was Hitler's first defeat of WWII; the wrecks of the sunk German destroyers are still on the fjord bed below you.

Twin

IMDb 7.1 · 2019 · Kristofer Hivju · NRK

Norwegian noir series filmed entirely in Lofoten — Reine, Hamnøy and Sørvågen. Kristofer Hivju (Tormund from Game of Thrones) plays identical twins in a village of a hundred people. Watch it before you go and you'll recognise every corner.
