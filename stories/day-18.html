Day 18 · Thu 15 Oct · Prague morning → Dresden

Stories, myths & legends

A porcelain mural that didn't burn when the whole city did. A green diamond nobody dares own. A king who bent horseshoes with his hands and (they claim) fathered 365 children. And a heist still fresh enough to be gossip.

The porcelain that didn't burn

Fürstenzug · 23,000 Meissen tiles · 1876

The Fürstenzug — Procession of Princes — is a 102-metre-long mural on the outside wall of the old royal stables, made of 23,000 fired Meissen porcelain tiles. In the February 1945 firebombing of Dresden the surrounding city burnt to the ground. The mural survived, unharmed, its glazed surface reflecting the flames back. The material explanation: fired porcelain doesn't burn. The Dresden explanation: God wanted the princes remembered. Both are on offer. The mural has never been restored — every tile you see is original, and older than the city around it.

The Dresden Green

Grünes Gewölbe · 41-carat green diamond

The Dresden Green Diamond is 41 carats — the largest natural-green diamond ever found (from Golconda, India, before 1725). Its colour comes from millions of years of natural radiation. Three of its 18th-century owners died within a few years of acquiring it. Augustus III of Saxony bought it in 1741 for the equivalent of five palaces. Since then it has been mounted, moved, hidden in a Königstein Fortress cellar during WWII, taken by the Red Army to Moscow, and returned in 1958. It has not been out of Saxony since. It is displayed today at the Grünes Gewölbe. Locals will tell you it is quietly cursed. Look at it. Notice you feel nothing. Or that you do.

Augustus the Strong

Augustus II · Elector of Saxony · King of Poland

Augustus II of Saxony ("the Strong") could — reliably, in front of witnesses — bend a horseshoe straight with his hands. He was said to have fathered 365 illegitimate children, one for each day of the year (the real number was probably eight to twelve, but the myth was too good to stop). He converted from Lutheranism to Catholicism in order to be elected King of Poland — a career move so cynical Voltaire admired it. He commissioned Meissen porcelain because he was obsessed with acquiring the Chinese secret; his alchemist Böttger cracked the formula in 1708. Every piece of Meissen you see in Dresden traces back to Augustus's obsession.

The Green Vault heist

Grünes Gewölbe · November 2019

On 25 November 2019 a group broke into the Green Vault at 4:57 a.m., cut a wire, smashed one vitrine with an axe, and took €113 million worth of 18th-century diamond jewellery. It took less than nine minutes. In late 2022 most of the pieces were recovered — battered, some broken, in a plastic bag traded for a plea deal. The Dresden Green was not touched; it was in a different display. Curators say the recovered pieces still show the axe marks. You can see them today. Not folklore, quite — living gossip.

The Yenidze — a cigarette factory in the shape of a mosque

Yenidze · 1909

In 1907 a Dresden cigarette-maker named Hugo Zietz wanted a factory that advertised itself as "oriental tobacco". City building regulations forbade an industrial chimney in the historic core. His solution: build the factory as a mosque — coloured glass dome, two minaret chimneys — and market it as "the Yenidze", after the Ottoman town where his tobacco came from. The city permitted it. Every Dresden clergy body protested. The chimneys, however, were legally minarets, and stayed. The factory closed in 1953. The building is now offices. The dome still lights up at night.

Told to the little one · Rumpelstiltskin

Grimm · Saxon folklore

A miller boasted his daughter could spin straw into gold. A king locked her in a room to prove it. A small strange man appeared, spun the straw into gold for her, and asked in return for her first child. She agreed. When the time came she begged him to release her. He said: "Only if you guess my name." She sent servants out to eavesdrop, they overheard him singing his own name at his fire — Rumpelstiltskin — and she named him. He was so furious he stamped his foot right through the floor. This is why: overhear the small men singing to themselves.
